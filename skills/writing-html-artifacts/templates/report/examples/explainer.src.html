When a payment call fails, checkout puts it on a retry queue instead of telling the shopper to try again. The queue waits longer between each attempt, gives up after five, and never charges the same order twice.

Applies to checkout service version 2.4 and later. Last reviewed 13 Oct 2026.

Why checkout retries at all

Payment providers fail for short, boring reasons: a dropped connection, a deploy on their side, a brief rate limit. About 2 in 100 payment calls fail this way, and about 9 in 10 of those succeed a few seconds later. Asking the shopper to press the button again turns a hiccup the shopper cannot fix into a lost sale.

So the service takes the failed call, tells the shopper the order is being confirmed, and tries again itself. The shopper sees a spinner for a few seconds instead of an error.

What happens to one failed call

A call that fails with a retryable error is written to the queue together with the order id and an idempotency key. A worker checks the queue every second, picks up an entry when its wait is over and calls the provider again.

Press "Step through" on the graph to follow one call from checkout to its end, either a retry that succeeds or one that gives up.

If the retry succeeds

The order moves to paid, the shopper's page updates, and the queue entry is deleted.

If it fails again

The wait doubles and the entry goes back on the queue. After the fifth failure the order moves to payment failed and the shopper gets an email with a link to pay another way.

How long it waits

Each wait is twice the one before, with a random spread of up to 20% so that a burst of failures does not return as a burst of retries.

Why the shopper is never charged twice

Every call carries the idempotency key stored with the queue entry. If the provider already processed a call whose answer was lost on the way back, it recognizes the key and returns the first result instead of charging again.

The key is created once, when the order is placed, and never regenerated by a retry. That is the single rule the whole design rests on.

def attempt(entry):
    result = provider.charge(entry.order_id, key=entry.idempotency_key)
    if result.ok:
        return mark_paid(entry)
    if entry.attempts >= 5 or not result.retryable:
        return mark_failed(entry)
    requeue(entry, wait=2 ** entry.attempts)

What one card payment goes through

Before a failed call ever reaches the queue, checkout has already handed the order to Billing, and Billing has asked Stripe to charge the card. Billing tries the charge up to 3 times, waits 2 seconds after a decline, and writes the payment to the ledger only once Stripe accepts it.

Press "Step through" to follow the order's payload message by message. "Card succeeds" is the plain case. "Declined, then retried" shows Stripe turning the card down once, Billing asking again, and the second attempt going through.

This loop is Billing's own, inside one payment request. It is not the retry queue, which never retries a decline.

What the queue does not cover

Declined cards are not retried, because a decline is an answer and not a failure. Calls that fail because the order itself is invalid are not retried either. Both go straight to payment failed with the provider's reason.

The queue also does not reorder anything. Two orders from the same shopper are retried independently, and either can finish first.

How to tell it is working

Three numbers on the checkout dashboard describe the queue: its depth, the age of its oldest entry, and the share of entries that end in payment failed. Depth under 50 and an oldest entry under 40 seconds is normal. A failure share above 10% for more than ten minutes pages the on-call engineer.

If the oldest entry keeps growing while the depth stays flat, workers are stuck rather than busy. Restarting the worker pool is safe, because every entry is persisted before it is attempted.
