## Observations

Lifecycle states and acknowledge

- S5, `references/media/incident-io/S5-docs-escalation-statuses.png`: alerts and escalations are separate objects. An alert has two statuses, Firing and Resolved. An escalation (who was paged, how, through which route and path) has more: Pending, Triggered, Acknowledged, Resolved, Expired, Cancelled, Snoozed, Delayed, Pending repeat. → feeds: command-center
- S5, section "Escalations": Pending is before anyone is notified; Triggered is actively moving through levels and unacknowledged; Expired means the whole path, with retries, ran with no acknowledgement; Cancelled means something else stopped it, such as the alert resolving; Delayed is a wait step with nobody notified; Pending repeat means the path ended while the alert still fires and it will restart. → feeds: command-center
- S5, section "Escalations": Acknowledged means someone on the path acknowledged. It does not advance to further levels, but it keeps paging anyone who has not yet acknowledged through their chosen contact methods; a level set to notify more than once stops retrying once anyone has acknowledged. A responder can still escalate manually to the next level. → feeds: command-center
- S5, section "Escalations": Resolved for an escalation is available only through the API; the dashboard shows those escalations as Acknowledged. With several people paged on one level, the escalation stays Acknowledged until everyone has acknowledged or declined, then becomes Resolved. → feeds: command-center
- S5, section "Important things to note": "You cannot acknowledge an alert, only an escalation", and an alert can only be resolved. The same split exists in PagerDuty (alerts are triggered or resolved), but there the unit a person acts on is the incident; here it is the escalation. → feeds: command-center

Snooze

- S4, section "How snoozing works": snoozing moves the escalation to the Snoozed status and pauses all notifications. When the duration ends it returns to Triggered and resumes. The timeline records who snoozed and for how long. Snoozing is offered from Slack, Microsoft Teams, the dashboard and the mobile app. → feeds: command-center
- S4, section "How snoozing works": "you'll be asked to provide a justification" when snoozing, so the team can see why it was deferred. The page does not say whether the field is required. → feeds: command-center
- S4, section "Snoozing vs acknowledging": acknowledging stops the current page but a path that repeats while the alert fires will page again at the repeat interval; snoozing pauses every page, repeats included. To silence it for good, resolve the alert. The FAQ confirms an already acknowledged escalation can also be snoozed, which differs from PagerDuty where snooze exists only on acknowledged incidents. → feeds: command-center
- S4, section "What happens while snoozed": it stays in the active escalation list, does not advance a level, and resumes at the same level when the snooze ends. If the route auto-resolves and the alert resolves during the snooze, the escalation resolves and no page is sent at expiry. A snooze counts as an acknowledgement for MTTA. → feeds: command-center
- S4, section FAQ: escalations that need multiple acknowledgers cannot be snoozed, because one person's snooze would pause everyone's notifications. Anyone with permission to view and respond can snooze, the same permission as acknowledging; it is not limited to the person paged. → feeds: command-center

Resolve and reopen

- S5, section "Resolving alerts": an alert is resolved from the alert detail page (top right), from Slack or Teams, by its source, or by an auto-resolve timeout that starts when the alert first fires. Some sources such as Grafana cannot be resolved manually because they send their own resolve event. → feeds: command-center
- S2, `references/media/incident-io/S2-s0540.png`: the alert detail header has an eye-icon "Inspect" button and a dark filled "Resolve alert" at the top right, next to a small red dot that shows the alert is still active. → feeds: command-center
- S3, `references/media/incident-io/S3-s0200.png`: resolving an incident (as opposed to an alert) opens a Slack modal "Resolve incident" with Name, an AI "Generating summary, this could take a few seconds..." field, Severity (Major, with a one-line description under it), Incident Lead, then Cancel and a green "Next" button, so resolving continues to another step. → feeds: command-center
- S5, section "Routing alerts": a route can auto-cancel escalations when its alert resolves, and can auto-decline a Triage incident if the alert resolves before it becomes a real incident. No source read here shows reopening an alert, escalation or incident. → feeds: command-center

Alert grouping into incidents

- S6, section "How it works": a route groups alerts by a time window, by matching attributes inside the window, or by AI similarity (beta). Windows are fixed (close a set time after the first alert) or extending (close a set time after the latest). A group takes its title and description from the first alert to join, except AI groups, which get a generated title. → feeds: command-center
- S6, section FAQ: the window is up to 48 hours, preselected at 30 minutes; a late alert starts a new group and, if the route creates incidents, a new incident. A group holds up to 1,000 alerts; groups cannot be closed by hand but close when the window expires or all attached incidents resolve. → feeds: command-center
- S6, section "Managing alerts in a group": "Ungroup" takes alerts out of a group, from the alert's page or a bulk bar, and then asks what the route would do with them: create a new incident (pre-filled, editable), merge into an existing one, or neither, and escalate to the people the route would have paged, or not. → feeds: command-center
- S2, `references/media/incident-io/S2-s0420.png`: the route builder is a vertical flow of four cards joined by thin connectors: "Receive alerts from" (three source chips), "Don't apply filters", "Escalate alerts to: Engineering", "Create incidents, and group alerts by: Time window (30 minutes)". An "Active" toggle and a greyed "Save changes" sit at the top right. → feeds: command-center
- S7, section "How does it work?": with auto-creation on, an incident is created in a Triage state. Joining it shows three choices: Accept (becomes a real incident, workflows start), Merge it into another, Decline (the incident is archived and excluded from metrics). Grouping suggestions in a channel offer Merge and Decline; "automatic grouping" attaches related alerts to the active incident without asking and can be undone with unlink. → feeds: command-center

## Patterns fed

- command-center: the full escalation vocabulary (Pending, Triggered, Acknowledged, Snoozed, Delayed, Pending repeat, Expired, Cancelled, Resolved) with acknowledge meaning "stop advancing" while snooze means "pause everything", snooze confirmed on an acknowledged escalation and framed on the page as the alternative to acknowledging a fresh page, a justification asked on snooze, and a snooze that counts as an acknowledgement; paging messages with three equal buttons (Acknowledge, I'm unavailable, Snooze) and a mobile sheet that shows a countdown to the next escalation level and a vertical event list; alerts grouped into one incident by window or attributes with an Ungroup action that previews what the route would have done; a timeline that records system events, waiting periods ("Allowing 2 minutes grace period before escalating") and hand-written notes, each with its own icon color, with a collapse-all control and a stated time zone; a Triage state that asks accept, merge or decline.
- inspector: the incident and alert sidebars of icon-plus-value rows (Status, Severity, Type, Duration; Roles; Links; Custom fields) beside a main column of description and timeline (S2).
