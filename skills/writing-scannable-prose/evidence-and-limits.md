# Evidence and limits

This file records what each of the 28 checks in [SKILL.md](SKILL.md) rests on, which four are `precautionary` rather than settled, the four falsification notes in full, the claims deliberately left out because they could not be sourced, and the gap this skill cannot close on its own. It is the citation layer — see "Pointer to the source research" below for the two documents every literature-backed claim here traces back to. Group F is the exception: F1–F4 were added by the 2026-07-30 amendment to the logic spec and rest on an observed failure in a worked example, not on anything in those explorations.

## Per-rule evidence status

`grounded` means the status carries no application-changing caveat — the check is used as its basis states. `precautionary` means the check rests on convergence across related findings rather than a single settled result, and carries a falsification note because its status does change how the check is applied — exactly four checks carry that status: **A4, B1, B3, C4**. Everything else below is grounded; a `grounded` row can still note a limitation on its citation, when that limitation does not change how the check is applied.

| Check | Status | Rests on |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | grounded | An observed failure in a worked example (2026-07-30 amendment), not a literature finding — the agent inferred the reader and decision silently and got section removal wrong on a real MR description. |
| F2 | grounded | Same worked-example failure: without a stated reader/decision, "load-bearing" had no referent and a section that should have gone stayed. |
| F3 | grounded | Same worked example: a section's bulk was routed out but two items were kept in a compressed shell, satisfying clause-grain checks while failing the reader — the residue-stub failure this check exists to prevent. |
| F4 | grounded | Same amendment; the category table names the recurring author-facing content the worked example surfaced, not a sourced taxonomy. |
| A1 | grounded | The financial-filings compression finding (context share 25%→9%, accuracy held constant) — naming the operation (word/claim/qualifier) surfaces qualifier-level cuts as the dangerous, invisible-in-diff case that finding documents. |
| A2 | grounded | Same finding — qualifiers were what compression dropped while claims stayed accurate, which is why a scope condition or sample size has to stay in the same visual unit as its claim rather than move to a footnote. |
| A3 | grounded | Same finding, applied to referential language: a claim can stay accurate while what it pointed at is gone, so a backward-pointing phrase or demonstrative needs a mechanical diff against the deletion range. |
| A4 | precautionary | The 2025 Google RCT (n=4,563: legal text +3.5%, PubMed +14.6%) shows content class decides compression payoff — that part is grounded. But the RCT tested two content types, not a spectrum, and does not establish that a passage-grain procedural/explanatory split — the classification this check actually makes — is the right cut boundary for technical prose. |
| A5 | grounded | The established concision literature (Zinsser, Williams, Lanham) on words that carry no information and have no destination — nominalizations, expletive constructions, redundant intensifiers. |
| A6 | grounded | Two independent bases: a materiality test (does omission change the reader's next action) and Diátaxis mode separation — both read as sound in the source review; the broader claim "most verbosity is misplacement" that the parent idea opened with did not hold up and is not part of this check. |
| B1 | precautionary | Millis, Golding & Barker 1995 (causal connectives like "because" trigger inference; "and"/"after" do not) plus Mayer's coherence principle (23/23 tests, median d=0.86). No clean bullets-vs-prose RCT on causal content exists — the closest available study confounds bullet removal with diagram addition — so the reversal test is convergence, not a direct result. |
| B2 | grounded | The same connectives finding, applied to labeling: an undecorated list reads as an implicit AND, so naming the relation ("choose one," "all of the following must hold") removes an ambiguity the bare glyph otherwise leaves. The connectives finding is about what causal connectives ("because" vs. "and"/"after") do cognitively; it does not itself measure how an unlabelled list reads. The extension from one to the other is an inference, not the study's own result. |
| B3 | precautionary | Parnas-style tabular expressions give completeness and consistency guarantees — grounded, but from formal requirements engineering for safety-critical specifications, a different genre and effort budget than a README or PR description. The banding and impossible/unspecified machinery built on top is reasoned extension, not independently sourced. |
| B4 | grounded | Mayer's multimedia principle (text+diagram beats text-alone, independent of bullet formatting) and the seductive-details effect (a decorative figure reliably hurts comprehension) — both real research lines. The specific effect-size numbers attached to them in the source material could not be verified against the research corpus and are stripped from this skill entirely, per Scope and non-goals. |
| B5 | grounded | Robillard's field study of Microsoft developers: code examples become a hindrance rather than a resource when a reader needs composition or design-rationale information a snippet was never built to carry. As with B4, no effect sizes ship — the qualitative distinction (mechanical vs. compositional/rationale questions) is what this check keeps. The Robillard citation itself could not be located in research.json or depth_repair.json, the research base this skill was built from — the qualitative distinction ships on the strength of the reasoning, not on a verified citation. |
| C1 | grounded | Gopen & Swan's topic/stress position framework — a sentence's final position is where new information lands; reading only the tail words of consecutive sentences surfaces whether a paragraph is advancing or stalling. |
| C2 | grounded | Same framework, plus Pullum's work on passive-voice misidentification: a passive sentence whose subject echoes the prior sentence is preserving a topic, not hiding an agent, which is why the check is a single lookup rather than a judgment call about vagueness. |
| C3 | grounded | The inverted-pyramid/BLUF convention, and Gopen & Swan's framework applied one level up — the same "old information first, new claim last" logic that governs a sentence also governs where a section puts its conclusion. |
| C4 | precautionary | The genre gate rests on Delgado et al.'s meta-analysis (screen deficit g=−0.27 for expository text, g=0.01 for narrative, 54 studies) — grounded. The structural trigger itself (an intervening heading, or a distant and ambiguous antecedent) has no direct measurement behind it; Cowan's ~4-chunk capacity ceiling is adjacent background — short-term recall of discrete stimuli under lab conditions with no rehearsal or external support — not a study of when a reader of continuous technical prose loses an antecedent, so it does not establish this check's trigger. |
| D1 | grounded | The serialization principle: visual emphasis must be lexically redundant with the words it marks, or a screen reader (or a plain-text extraction) loses it entirely. |
| D2 | grounded | The same Google RCT differential behind A4 (legal vs. PubMed) read as a stakes finding rather than a content-shape finding: what a misread costs, not how important a claim feels, is what should earn a section's emphasis. |
| D3 | grounded | WebAIM's 2024 survey: 71.6% of screen-reader users navigate long pages primarily by headings, which makes the heading outline a primary index rather than decoration. |
| D4 | grounded | The same serialization principle as D1, plus WCAG's requirement that meaning not depend on color, position, or an emoji a screen reader announces literally rather than interprets. |
| D5 | grounded | The resolution of the Miller-vs-Cowan disagreement: the two capacity numbers were measured under different support conditions, so list capacity is a consequence of which channel (visually re-consultable vs. unaided/linear) the reader is in, not a fixed count either number could settle on its own. |
| D6 | grounded | WCAG's table requirements (header cells with `scope`, a caption) and the documented failure mode where responsive reflow strips header association, leaving a sequence of unlabeled values. |
| E1 | grounded | Convergent practitioner reports that stale documentation reads exactly like current documentation, with no signal telling a reader which fact was the volatile one — the basis for tagging facts stable vs. perishable rather than trusting review cadence to catch drift. |
| E2 | grounded | The same practitioner material, applied to runnable content: an executable check is the one enforcement mechanism in this material that does not decay when nobody happens to be watching — while it stays enabled; the check itself can still be disabled, narrowed, or skipped. |
| E3 | grounded | Martinez, Mollica & Gibson 2024 — 50 years of plain-language mandates produced no measurable decline in processing-difficulty features — plus the ACL 2021 GEM critique showing a readability-formula score can move the wrong direction relative to genuine simplification. A proxy that can move opposite to the thing it is meant to measure cannot be a gate. |

## Falsification notes

A falsification note states what result would show the rule wrong, concretely enough to recognize. These are the full versions; `SKILL.md` carries one line per note and links here.

**A4 — cut license by content class.** Would be disproven if two independent reviewers, given the same passage, classify it procedural vs. explanatory at passage grain and disagree for a nontrivial share of ordinary technical-prose passages — not a cherry-picked example. That would show the classification does not converge and is not reviewer-runnable as stated. Separately, if a passage cut hard toward bare imperatives under the procedural license was found to have dropped a trade-off, scope condition, sample size, or limitation — the one thing the check requires survive regardless of content class — the license would be falsified outright, not merely weakened.

**B1 — reversal test.** Would be disproven if a real passage is found where reversing two items changes what is true or what a reader could correctly do, yet a *because*-class connective demonstrably adds nothing a reader needed — showing the reversal test flags pairs that do not actually need a causal connective. Separately, if independent reviewers applying the reversal test to the same disputed pair disagree on whether reversal changes truth or correct action for a nontrivial share of real passages, the test does not converge, and the rule loses its claim to being resolvable without a second decision layer.

**B3 — table as completeness proof.** Would be disproven if an audit of real READMEs, ADRs, guides, and PR descriptions shows that two or more independent, orthogonal conditions actually determining a stated outcome — the condition that triggers gridding — is rare in these genres, as opposed to common in the formal safety-critical specifications the underlying completeness guarantee was built for. That would mean the check addresses a corner case dressed as a general prose rule. Separately, if writers under real deadline pressure default to marking an unfillable cell IMPOSSIBLE on intuition rather than tracking down a citable invariant elsewhere in the document, the impossible/unspecified distinction has no teeth without tooling to enforce the citation, and collapses back into the undocumented gap it exists to surface.

**C4 — restate only where a reader plausibly lost the thread.** Would be disproven if a reading-comprehension or eye-tracking study on technical prose showed readers do not lose the thread across an intervening heading, or do not lose it when the antecedent is distant and ambiguous — undercutting the structural trigger this check actually fires on. Separately, if server-log or analytics data from an actual technical-docs site showed search-referral share for reference and how-to pages is not meaningfully higher than for tutorial pages, that would undercut the differential arrival-mode scoping this check uses to restate aggressively on reference and how-to content, lightly on tutorial, and never on changelog entries.

## Claims deliberately not cited

These circulated widely enough in the source research to be tempting and were deliberately excluded because they could not be sourced. `SKILL.md` inlines the first five as a do-not-cite blocklist; this is the full set.

- **The 30%-bold maximum.** Appears in a formatting article without a cited study behind it.
- **"Users read 25% faster with bullets."** The nearest real measurement found was a different metric — "21% higher skimmability" — not a reading-speed figure; the 25% number itself was not traced to a source.
- **"Bold enables 30% faster scanning."** Circulates widely; no primary source located.
- **7±2 as a list cap.** The actual research is a 2001 revision of the original figure down to ~4, scoped to short-term recall of discrete stimuli under lab conditions with no rehearsal or external support. Most sources still citing 7 are citing a number the field itself already revised, and even the revised figure measures a different task than list length in technical prose — neither number is a citable cap.
- **The F-pattern as a design target.** The research that named it defines it as the default pattern when there are no strong cues to attract the eye toward meaningful information, and a larger 99-site eyetracking study found no consistent letter-shaped pattern at all — a center-left blob instead. Designing for the F-pattern optimizes for the absence of formatting cues, not for a target worth building toward.
- **Shannon's redundancy estimate** for English (roughly 50-75%). No primary source retrieved.
- **Lin 1999's optimal compression band** (roughly 15-30%). The paper is real; the specific figure could not be confirmed in the primary text.
- **Carroll's minimalism task-time figures.** Consistently described in secondary literature; the primary source was not retrieved.

## The real-user gap

None of the 28 checks — including the ones marked grounded on the strength of a controlled study — has been validated by testing this skill's revised output against real readers. The literature behind them measured something else: general prose, financial filings, legislative text, lab-controlled multimedia materials, screen-reader survey respondents. None of it measured a document this skill actually revised, read by a person deciding whether the revision helped or hurt.

That is the same gap the source material names as the missing "second reader": every judgment-tier check assumes an independent rater who can contest the author's own classification — catch an orphaned claim, a mistagged clause, a table that obscures a distinction the prose stated clearly. Inside a single agent session no such rater exists. The agent classifies, cuts, and checks its own work; a structural self-check is a substitute for outside judgment, not a proof the judgment was right. This cannot be closed by more research or a better self-check protocol — it needs an experiment with actual readers of actual revised documents, which cannot happen inside an authoring or revision session.

Consequence: every line in the per-rule table above, including every "grounded" one, is a claim that a mechanism plausible from the literature was applied — not a claim that applying it measurably improved comprehension for a reader of this skill's output. Treat the rule set as well-reasoned inference until that gap closes, not as a validated intervention.

## Why no numeric thresholds

This skill ships no numeric thresholds anywhere in its 28 checks — no bold-density ceiling, no list-length cap, no compression percentage. Two sourced findings in the research corpus look like they could supply one, and neither can bear that weight.

The ~4-chunk capacity ceiling measured short-term recall of discrete stimuli under lab conditions with no rehearsal or external support — a specific experimental task, not a measurement of how many list items or intervening named entities a reader of continuous technical prose can track before losing an antecedent. The 71.6% figure describes how screen-reader users navigate pages today; it supports treating the heading outline as a primary index, not a percentage anything else gets checked against.

Turning either into a rule's target would be the same construct-validity leap that produced the claims listed above as unverifiable: a plausible number borrowed from an adjacent measurement, applied to a different task, repeated until nobody remembers the derivation broke on the way over. A threshold this skill invented to fill the gap would carry a shorter, less examined pedigree than the numbers it would replace.
